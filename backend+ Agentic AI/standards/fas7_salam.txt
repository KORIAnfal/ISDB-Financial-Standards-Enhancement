
AAOIFI Financial Accounting Standard No. 7 (FAS 7): Salam and Parallel Salam

1. Scope of the Standard:
This standard addresses the accounting rules for Salam financing and Parallel Salam transactions, including:
- Treatment of capital (Ra’s Al-Mal) paid/received in Salam or Parallel Salam.
- Receipt and sale of Al-Muslam Fihi (the subject of the contract).
- Revenues, expenses, gains, and losses arising from such transactions.
Disclosure must be made if this standard conflicts with the bank’s charter or national laws.

2. Accounting Treatments:

2.1 Recognition:
- Salam financing is recognized when the capital is paid or made available to Al-Muslam Ileihi.
- Parallel Salam is recognized when the capital is received by the Islamic bank.

2.2 At Contracting Time:
a) Cash capital is measured by the actual amount paid.
b) Non-cash capital (kind or benefit) is measured at fair value agreed upon.

2.3 At Financial Period End:
a) Salam capital remains as per item 2.2 unless probable delivery failure or decline in value occurs, in which case a provision must be made.
b) Salam transactions are shown as assets (Salam Financing).
c) Parallel Salam transactions are shown as liabilities (Parallel Salam).

2.4 Receipt of Al-Muslam Fihi:
- Received goods are recorded at historical cost.
- If quality differs:
  - Equal market/book value: record at book value.
  - Lower market value: record at market value and recognize loss.
- If not received:
  - If extended: retain book value.
  - If cancelled and capital not repaid: recognize as receivable.

2.5 Client Misconduct:
- Recognize receivables if Salam is cancelled due to client negligence or non-repayment.
- Any pledged security shortfall becomes a receivable; surplus returned to client.
- Additional amounts due to misconduct are recorded as receivables.

2.6 Substitution:
- If substitute goods are of lower value than Al-Muslam Fihi, record at market/fair value and recognize loss.

2.7 Post-Receipt Measurement:
- Use lower of historical cost or cash equivalent at period-end; loss recognized if lower.

2.8 Parallel Salam Delivery:
- Difference between cost of goods delivered and amount received from client = profit/loss.

3. Disclosure Requirements:
Disclose as per AAOIFI FAS 1: General Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements of Islamic Banks.

4. Effective Date:
Applies to financial periods beginning 1 Muharram 1419 A.H. / 1 January 1999 A.D.

Juristic Foundations of Salam and Parallel Salam:

1. Definition:
Salam is the sale of a commodity for deferred delivery in exchange for immediate payment.

2. Legitimacy:
- Based on Qur’an, Sunnah, and scholarly consensus (Ijma’).
- Supported by necessity: Salam provides liquidity to producers (e.g., farmers, traders).

3. Capital of Salam:
- Must be known, paid in full, preferably in cash.
- Permitted in kind or benefit (e.g., housing) by some schools (e.g., Malikis).

4. Al-Muslam Fihi:
- Must be standardized in kind, type, quality, and amount.
- Delivery must be deferred and date fixed.
- Must not be tied to uncertain events or specific sources (e.g., one farm).

5. Delivery and Sale:
- Delivery must occur at a mutually agreed place; if unspecified, default to custom.
- Resale of Al-Muslam Fihi before receipt is not allowed (except with exceptions by Maliki school).

6. Replacement/Substitution:
- Only allowed if substitute is same/lower quality and quantity does not exceed original.
- Prohibited if it leads to Riba or debt-for-debt transaction.

7. Parallel Salam:
- Permissible if the two contracts (buy and sell) are separate and independent.
- Some scholars prohibit it if it becomes a regular trading mechanism.

8. General Rules:
- Complete or partial cancellation is permissible.
- Early delivery is allowed only if quality and quantity match.
- Inferior delivery may be accepted without price reduction.

9. Non-Delivery:
- Al-Muslam may cancel the contract or wait until delivery is possible.

10. Transferability:
- Al-Muslam Fihi may be pledged or transferred as it is considered a debt instrument.
